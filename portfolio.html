01

AWS Marketplace Storefronts for 35+ software companies

  • Role Designer and builder; partner onboarding
  • When Jun–Oct 2026
  • Used AWS Marketplace, AWS Partner Central, Claude

Problem

A Marketplace listing is one product page. Software companies with several products had no branded place where an AWS buyer could see everything they sell, filter it and buy it through AWS.

Approach

Design each Storefront from the company's own website and listings: decide the layout, branding, categories, filters and featured products, and make Buy with AWS the action on every product.

Execution

Built the Storefronts and guided each partner's AWS admin through the CloudFormation set-up and invitations. Moved one large software company to a standalone layout so buyers could search its catalogue of about 100 products. Wrote a 13-step onboarding and integration guide, wrote an AI prompt that turns a company website into a first Storefront configuration draft, and trained a colleague to design them.

Outcome

Storefronts designed for 35+ software companies, with a repeatable process (guide, prompt, trained colleague) instead of one person's know-how.

Review note: add how many of the 35+ are live today, and any buyer-side metric you are allowed to share.

02

Finding why partner revenue showed $0, and escalating it to AWS

  • Role Self-initiated audit and write-up
  • When 2026
  • Used AWS Marketplace seller data, Google Sheets

Problem

Partner accounts were showing $0 in billed revenue. If the number is wrong, every revenue report and update built on it is wrong too.

Approach

Nobody had asked for it, so I treated it as an audit: check every partner account rather than the one that looked broken, and compare offer records with what had been agreed.

Execution

Audited billed-revenue data across all partner accounts and six private-offer records against agreement records. Put the affected accounts in one list with the evidence and escalated it to AWS. Fixed one partner's missing data-feed set-up (an SNS notification topic) live on a call, then wrote the seller-account configuration SOP.

Outcome

AWS received one list with the evidence behind it, and the team got a written set-up procedure to follow for every new partner account.

Review note: add what AWS changed after the escalation, if you can share it.

03

AWS Competency evidence and customer case studies

  • Role Prepared the documentation; the CEO submitted it
  • When Mar–Oct 2026
  • Used AWS Partner Central, AWS competency checklists

Problem

An AWS Competency brings AWS funding, co-sell support and a badge buyers trust. But AWS reviewers check every claim against a fixed list, and a strong story without proof gets sent back.

Approach

Write case studies the way a reviewer reads them: the customer's problem, what was built and on which AWS services, and an outcome a reviewer can check. Close every gap a reviewer flags, not just the easy ones.

Execution

Prepared the self-assessment and fact-checked case studies for SaaSNova's AWS AI Services Competency. Fixed all 43 deficiencies in an ISV client's AWS AI Competency self-assessment between 23 and 30 June. Wrote GenAI, Security and SaaS competency plans for five ISVs, plus the company's AWS case-study template.

Outcome

AWS approved SaaSNova's AI Services Competency. 43 of 43 deficiencies closed in about a week. 17+ case studies across 9 partner accounts.
